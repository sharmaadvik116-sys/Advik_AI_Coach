# Measurements in Remote Sensing

## What Are Measurements?

Remote sensing data can be used to measure or estimate physical properties of Earth's surface.

Common measurements include:

- Distance
- Area
- Perimeter
- Elevation
- Slope
- Change over time

Measurements are useful because they allow scientists to turn images and maps into quantitative information.

---

# Distance

Distance describes how far apart two locations or features are.

Distance can be measured:

- On a map
- In a satellite image
- Between geographic coordinates
- Between points on Earth's surface

The units depend on the scale of the measurement.

Common units include:

- meters (m)
- kilometers (km)
- feet (ft)
- miles (mi)

---

## Map Scale and Distance

Map scale can be used to convert a map distance into a real-world distance.

For a ratio scale:

1:N

one unit on the map represents N of the same units on the ground.

### Example

Suppose a map has a scale of:

1:50,000

If two locations are 4 cm apart on the map:

4 × 50,000 = 200,000 cm

Convert centimeters to kilometers:

200,000 cm = 2 km

Therefore, the real-world distance is:

2 km

---

# Area

Area describes the amount of surface covered by a feature.

Common area units include:

- square meters (m²)
- square kilometers (km²)
- square feet (ft²)
- square miles (mi²)
- hectares

Remote sensing can be used to estimate the area of:

- Forests
- Lakes
- Agricultural fields
- Urban areas
- Burned areas
- Flooded areas

---

## Area of a Rectangle

For a rectangular area:

A = length × width

### Example

A field is:

200 m long

and:

100 m wide

Then:

A = 200 × 100

A = 20,000 m²

---

# Pixel-Based Area

A remote sensing image is made up of pixels.

If the ground area represented by each pixel is known, the number of pixels can be used to estimate the area of a feature.

### Example

Suppose each pixel represents:

10 m × 10 m

The area represented by one pixel is:

10 × 10 = 100 m²

If a feature covers 500 pixels:

500 × 100 = 50,000 m²

Therefore, the estimated area is:

50,000 m²

---

# Perimeter

Perimeter is the total distance around the boundary of a feature.

For a rectangle:

P = 2L + 2W

Where:

- P = perimeter
- L = length
- W = width

### Example

A rectangular lake is:

300 m long

and:

100 m wide.

P = 2(300) + 2(100)

P = 600 + 200

P = 800 m

---

# Elevation

Elevation describes the height of a location relative to a reference surface.

Elevation data can come from remote sensing systems and can be represented using:

- Contour maps
- Digital Elevation Models (DEMs)
- Elevation maps
- Radar-derived elevation products

Elevation is important for understanding Earth's terrain.

---

# Digital Elevation Models

A Digital Elevation Model (DEM) represents elevation digitally across an area.

A DEM can be used to study:

- Mountains
- Valleys
- Slopes
- Drainage
- Terrain shape
- Elevation differences

DEM data can also support calculations of slope and other terrain characteristics.

---

# Slope

Slope describes how quickly elevation changes over a horizontal distance.

A simple slope calculation is:

slope = change in elevation / horizontal distance

or:

slope = Δelevation / Δdistance

---

## Example

Suppose elevation increases by:

200 m

over a horizontal distance of:

1,000 m

Then:

slope = 200 / 1,000

slope = 0.20

As a percentage:

0.20 × 100 = 20%

Therefore, the slope is:

20%

---

# Gradient

Gradient is another way to describe the rate of change of a quantity over distance.

For terrain:

gradient can describe the change in elevation relative to horizontal distance.

A steeper slope has a larger elevation change over the same horizontal distance.

---

# Contour Lines and Slope

Contour lines can provide visual information about slope.

### Closely spaced contour lines

Usually indicate:

**Steep terrain**

### Widely spaced contour lines

Usually indicate:

**Gentler terrain**

The spacing between contours helps show how quickly elevation changes across an area.

---

# Measuring Change

Remote sensing can compare observations collected at different times.

This is called change detection.

Examples include:

- Forest loss
- Urban growth
- Flood expansion
- Burned areas
- Vegetation changes
- Surface deformation

A simple change measurement can be:

change = later measurement - earlier measurement

---

## Example

Suppose an urban area covered:

100 km²

in an earlier image.

Later it covered:

125 km²

Change:

125 - 100 = 25 km²

The urban area increased by:

25 km²

---

# Percentage Change

Percentage change can be calculated using:

percentage change = (change / original value) × 100

### Example

Original forest area:

500 km²

Later forest area:

450 km²

Change:

450 - 500 = -50 km²

Percentage change:

(-50 / 500) × 100

= -10%

The negative value indicates a decrease.

Therefore, forest area decreased by:

10%

---

# Surface Deformation

Some remote sensing techniques can detect changes in Earth's surface position.

InSAR can be used to study surface deformation.

Applications include:

- Subsidence
- Earthquake-related deformation
- Volcanic activity
- Ground movement

Small changes in surface position can sometimes be detected by comparing radar observations.

---

# Measurement Units

Common remote sensing measurements may use:

| Quantity | Common Units |
|---|---|
| Distance | m, km |
| Area | m², km² |
| Elevation | m, km |
| Slope | ratio or % |
| Temperature | °C or K |
| Time | seconds, days |
| Wavelength | nm, µm, m |

Always check the units before performing a calculation.

---

# Unit Conversion

Some common relationships:

1 km = 1,000 m

1 m = 100 cm

1 km = 100,000 cm

1 m² = 10,000 cm²

When converting units, make sure the units match before performing calculations.

---

# Measurement Strategy

When solving a remote sensing measurement problem:

### Step 1

Identify what is being measured.

Is it:

- Distance?
- Area?
- Perimeter?
- Elevation?
- Slope?
- Change?

### Step 2

Identify the given values.

### Step 3

Check the units.

### Step 4

Choose the appropriate equation.

### Step 5

Substitute the values.

### Step 6

Calculate carefully.

### Step 7

Include the correct units in the final answer.
