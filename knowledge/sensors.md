# Remote Sensing Sensors

## What Is a Remote Sensing Sensor?

A remote sensing sensor is an instrument that detects and records electromagnetic energy from Earth's surface or atmosphere.

Different sensors are designed to detect different types of energy.

The type of sensor determines what information can be collected.

---

## Two Main Types of Sensors

Remote sensing sensors can generally be divided into:

1. Passive sensors
2. Active sensors

---

# Passive Sensors

A passive sensor detects naturally occurring electromagnetic energy.

Passive sensors do not send their own energy toward the target.

One important source of energy is the Sun.

Sunlight can interact with Earth's surface and then be reflected toward a sensor.

Passive sensors can also detect thermal energy emitted by Earth's surface.

---

## Optical Sensors

Optical sensors detect electromagnetic radiation in portions of the electromagnetic spectrum.

Important wavelength regions include:

- Ultraviolet (UV)
- Visible
- Near-infrared (NIR)
- Shortwave infrared (SWIR)
- Thermal infrared

Different materials reflect and emit different amounts of energy at different wavelengths.

This allows optical and infrared sensors to help distinguish different materials and surface conditions.

---

## Visible Light

Visible light is the portion of the electromagnetic spectrum that human eyes can detect.

It includes colors such as:

- Blue
- Green
- Red

Natural-color imagery generally combines visible bands to create an image that looks similar to what humans see.

---

## Near-Infrared

Near-infrared (NIR) radiation is outside the range visible to human eyes.

Vegetation often reflects strongly in the near-infrared portion of the spectrum.

Because of this, NIR measurements can be useful for studying vegetation.

---

## Shortwave Infrared

Shortwave infrared (SWIR) is another portion of the electromagnetic spectrum used in remote sensing.

SWIR measurements can provide information about surface materials and moisture conditions.

Different materials can have different responses in SWIR wavelengths.

---

## Thermal Infrared

Thermal infrared sensors detect emitted thermal radiation.

Objects at different temperatures emit different amounts of thermal energy.

Thermal infrared measurements can therefore be used to study surface temperature.

---

# Active Sensors

An active sensor produces and sends its own energy toward a target.

The sensor then measures the returned signal.

Radar is an important example of an active remote sensing system.

One advantage of active sensing is that it does not depend on sunlight for illumination.

---

# Radar

Radar stands for:

Radio Detection and Ranging

Radar sends electromagnetic energy toward a target and measures the returned signal.

The returned signal is sometimes called the backscatter.

The strength and characteristics of the returned signal depend on properties of the target and the interaction between the radar signal and the surface.

---

## Radar Backscatter

Backscatter is the portion of transmitted radar energy that is scattered back toward the radar sensor.

Different surfaces can produce different amounts of backscatter.

Backscatter can be affected by factors such as:

- Surface roughness
- Moisture
- Geometry
- Material properties
- Radar wavelength
- Viewing angle

A radar image can therefore contain information that is different from a visible-light image.

---

# Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR)

SAR stands for:

Synthetic Aperture Radar

SAR is an active remote sensing system that uses radar.

SAR can produce detailed images of Earth's surface.

An important feature of SAR is that it can collect data during both day and night.

Because radar provides its own illumination, SAR does not require sunlight to create an image.

SAR can also be useful when clouds or other atmospheric conditions make optical observations difficult.

---

# InSAR

InSAR stands for:

Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar

InSAR uses radar observations and differences in the radar signal phase to study changes in the Earth's surface.

InSAR can be used to detect very small changes in surface elevation or position.

Applications can include studying:

- Ground deformation
- Subsidence
- Earthquake-related changes
- Volcanic activity
- Other surface movements

---

# Radar Altimetry

Radar altimetry uses radar to measure the distance between a sensor and a surface.

A radar altimeter sends a radar pulse toward a surface and measures the time required for the signal to return.

The travel time can be used to estimate the distance between the sensor and the surface.

Radar altimetry can be used to study:

- Ocean surface height
- Ice surfaces
- Land elevation
- Other surface-height measurements

---

## Basic Radar Range Relationship

A radar signal travels from the sensor to the target and back.

The basic relationship is:

R = ct/2

Where:

- R = range or distance to the target
- c = speed of light
- t = total round-trip travel time

The division by 2 is necessary because the measured time includes both:

1. The trip from the sensor to the target
2. The trip from the target back to the sensor

---

## Radar-Derived Elevation Products

Radar measurements can be processed to produce information about elevation.

Elevation products can help represent the height and shape of Earth's surface.

Digital elevation products can be used to study:

- Terrain
- Mountains
- Valleys
- Slopes
- Surface changes

---

# Passive vs. Active Sensors

| Feature | Passive Sensor | Active Sensor |
|---|---|---|
| Provides its own energy? | No | Yes |
| Detects naturally available energy? | Yes | Can detect returned energy |
| Example | Optical sensor | Radar |
| Requires sunlight? | Some passive systems depend on sunlight | No |
| Can operate at night? | Depends on the type | Yes for radar |
| Measures reflected energy? | Often | Measures returned signal |

# Why Sensor Type Matters

Different sensors provide different types of information.

For example:

### Optical sensor

Can provide information about reflected visible and infrared radiation.

### Thermal sensor

Can provide information about emitted thermal radiation and surface temperature.

### Radar

Can provide information about returned microwave energy and surface characteristics.

### SAR

Can produce radar images and support measurements of surface changes.

### Radar altimeter

Can measure the distance between the sensor and a surface.

Choosing the appropriate sensor depends on the scientific question being studied.

---

# Quick Review

### Passive sensor

Detects naturally available electromagnetic energy.

### Active sensor

Sends its own energy toward a target and measures the returned signal.

### Radar

Uses electromagnetic energy to detect and measure targets.

### Backscatter

Radar energy that is scattered back toward the sensor.

### SAR

Synthetic Aperture Radar.

### InSAR

Interferometric Synthetic Aperture Radar, which can be used to study surface deformation.

### Radar altimetry

Uses radar travel time to measure distance between a sensor and a surface.
