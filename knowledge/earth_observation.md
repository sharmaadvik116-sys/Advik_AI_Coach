# Earth Observation in Remote Sensing

## What Is Earth Observation?

Earth observation is the collection and analysis of information about Earth's surface, atmosphere, and processes.

Remote sensing allows scientists to observe large areas of Earth without physically visiting every location.

Earth observation can help scientists monitor:

- Atmosphere
- Oceans
- Land
- Vegetation
- Surface temperature
- Elevation
- Natural hazards
- Environmental change

---

# Aerosols

Aerosols are tiny particles or droplets suspended in the atmosphere.

Examples include:

- Dust
- Smoke
- Sea salt
- Sulfates
- Other atmospheric particles

Aerosols can affect how electromagnetic radiation travels through the atmosphere.

They can:

- Scatter radiation
- Absorb radiation
- Change the amount of energy reaching Earth's surface
- Affect what a remote sensing sensor detects

---

# Atmospheric Scattering

Scattering occurs when electromagnetic radiation changes direction because it interacts with particles or molecules.

Atmospheric scattering can affect remote sensing observations.

The amount and type of scattering depend on factors such as:

- Particle size
- Wavelength
- Atmospheric conditions

Scattering can make some wavelengths appear brighter or alter the signal received by a sensor.

---

# Atmospheric Absorption

Atmospheric gases can absorb certain wavelengths of electromagnetic radiation.

This means that not all electromagnetic energy emitted or reflected from Earth's surface reaches a sensor equally.

Different gases absorb different portions of the electromagnetic spectrum.

Atmospheric absorption is therefore important when selecting wavelengths for remote sensing.

---

# Atmospheric Windows

An atmospheric window is a wavelength range where electromagnetic radiation can pass through the atmosphere relatively well.

Remote sensing instruments often use wavelength regions that provide useful atmospheric transmission.

Atmospheric windows allow sensors to collect information from Earth's surface that might otherwise be strongly absorbed by the atmosphere.

---

# Albedo

Albedo describes the fraction of incoming radiation that a surface reflects.

A surface with high albedo reflects a relatively large portion of incoming radiation.

A surface with low albedo absorbs a relatively larger portion of incoming radiation.

Examples of surfaces with relatively high reflectivity can include:

- Snow
- Ice
- Some clouds

Darker surfaces can have lower albedo.

---

# Why Albedo Matters

Albedo affects Earth's energy balance.

If a surface reflects more incoming solar radiation, less energy is absorbed by that surface.

If a surface reflects less radiation, more energy can be absorbed.

Remote sensing can be used to study differences and changes in surface reflectance and albedo.

---

# Surface Temperature

Remote sensing can measure or estimate Earth's surface temperature using thermal infrared observations.

Different surfaces can have different temperatures.

Examples include:

- Water
- Vegetation
- Soil
- Roads
- Buildings

Thermal observations can reveal patterns that are not always visible in ordinary photographs.

---

# Urban Heat

Cities can contain large areas of:

- Buildings
- Roads
- Parking lots
- Other human-made surfaces

These surfaces can have different thermal characteristics from surrounding vegetation and rural areas.

Thermal remote sensing can be used to study patterns of surface temperature in urban areas.

---

# Vegetation Changes

Remote sensing is useful for monitoring vegetation over large areas.

Scientists can observe changes such as:

- Vegetation growth
- Vegetation loss
- Deforestation
- Drought effects
- Seasonal changes
- Agricultural changes
- Fire damage

Different wavelengths can provide different information about vegetation condition.

---

# Vegetation and Near-Infrared Radiation

Healthy vegetation often reflects strongly in the near-infrared portion of the electromagnetic spectrum.

Vegetation also absorbs much of the visible red light used in photosynthesis.

The difference between visible and near-infrared responses can therefore provide useful information about vegetation.

This is one reason multispectral remote sensing is useful for studying vegetation.

---

# Surface Reflectance

Surface reflectance describes the fraction of incoming radiation that is reflected by a surface.

Different surfaces can have different reflectance characteristics.

Reflectance can vary with:

- Wavelength
- Surface material
- Moisture
- Vegetation condition
- Surface roughness
- Viewing and illumination conditions

Remote sensing can measure reflected energy to help identify and study surface materials.

---

# Elevation and Surface Change

Remote sensing can provide information about Earth's elevation.

Elevation data can be used to study:

- Mountains
- Valleys
- Slopes
- Terrain
- Surface deformation

Changes in elevation can sometimes indicate processes occurring at Earth's surface.

---

# Subsidence

Subsidence is the downward movement or sinking of Earth's surface.

Possible causes include:

- Groundwater withdrawal
- Natural geological processes
- Underground resource extraction
- Soil compaction

Remote sensing techniques such as InSAR can help detect surface deformation associated with subsidence.

---

# Geohazards

Geohazards are natural processes that can create hazards for people or the environment.

Examples include:

- Earthquakes
- Volcanoes
- Landslides
- Floods
- Wildfires

Remote sensing can help scientists observe and map areas affected by these events.

---

# Earthquakes

Earthquakes can cause changes in Earth's surface.

Remote sensing can be used to:

- Map affected areas
- Compare images before and after an event
- Detect surface deformation
- Identify damaged infrastructure

InSAR can be particularly useful for studying ground deformation.

---

# Volcanoes

Remote sensing can help monitor volcanic activity.

Possible observations include:

- Thermal anomalies
- Changes in surface elevation
- Lava flows
- Ash plumes
- Changes around volcanic areas

Thermal sensors can detect unusually warm surfaces associated with volcanic activity.

---

# Floods

Remote sensing can help identify flooded areas.

Flood mapping can use different types of data, including:

- Optical imagery
- Radar imagery

Radar can be especially useful because it can collect observations without depending on sunlight.

---

# Wildfires

Remote sensing can help detect and study wildfires.

Possible observations include:

- Thermal anomalies
- Burned areas
- Smoke
- Vegetation loss
- Changes in land cover

Thermal observations can help identify areas with unusually high surface temperatures.

---

# Earth Processes

Earth is constantly changing.

Remote sensing can help scientists study processes such as:

- Erosion
- Sedimentation
- Vegetation change
- Surface deformation
- Volcanic activity
- Flooding
- Fire
- Urban growth
- Land-cover change

Repeated observations are especially useful because they allow scientists to compare conditions over time.

---

# Multi-Temporal Observations

Multi-temporal remote sensing means using observations collected at different times.

For example:

Image 1 → January

Image 2 → June

Image 3 → December

Comparing these images can reveal changes.

This is closely related to temporal resolution and change detection.

---

# Connecting Sensors to Earth Observation

Different sensors are useful for different Earth observation questions.

### Optical sensors

Useful for reflected visible and infrared energy.

### Thermal sensors

Useful for studying emitted thermal radiation and surface temperature.

### Radar

Useful for studying returned microwave signals and surface characteristics.

### SAR

Useful for radar imaging and surface deformation studies.

### Radar altimetry

Useful for measuring distance to surfaces and studying surface height.

---

# Earth Observation Strategy

When analyzing an Earth observation problem, ask:

1. What phenomenon are we trying to observe?
2. What sensor is being used?
3. What wavelength or type of energy is being measured?
4. Is the sensor passive or active?
5. What property of Earth's surface or atmosphere affects the signal?
6. What type of image or measurement is produced?
7. How can the data be interpreted?
8. Is comparison over time useful?
