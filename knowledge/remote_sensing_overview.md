# Remote Sensing Overview

## What Is Remote Sensing?

Remote sensing is the process of collecting information about an object, area, or Earth's surface without physically touching it.

A remote sensing system usually involves:

1. A source of energy
2. Energy traveling through the atmosphere
3. Interaction between the energy and Earth's surface
4. A sensor detecting the returned or reflected energy
5. Data being processed into images or other useful information

Satellites and aircraft can carry sensors that collect information about Earth's surface.

---

## Why Is Remote Sensing Useful?

Remote sensing allows scientists to study large areas and observe changes over time.

It can be used to study:

- Vegetation
- Water
- Land cover
- Surface temperature
- Elevation
- Urban areas
- Natural hazards
- Environmental changes

Remote sensing is especially useful when an area is difficult or unsafe to visit directly.

---

## Passive and Active Remote Sensing

There are two major types of remote sensing systems.

### Passive Remote Sensing

Passive sensors detect naturally available energy.

For example, sunlight can reflect from Earth's surface and be detected by a sensor.

Thermal sensors can detect thermal energy emitted by objects and Earth's surface.

Examples of passive sensing include:

- Visible imagery
- Near-infrared imagery
- Shortwave infrared imagery
- Thermal infrared measurements

### Active Remote Sensing

Active sensors send their own energy toward a target and measure the returned signal.

Radar is an example of an active remote sensing system.

Because active sensors provide their own energy source, they can operate without relying on sunlight.

---

## Electromagnetic Energy

Remote sensing commonly uses electromagnetic radiation.

Different wavelengths interact with Earth's surface in different ways.

Important portions of the electromagnetic spectrum include:

- Ultraviolet (UV)
- Visible light
- Near-infrared (NIR)
- Shortwave infrared (SWIR)
- Thermal infrared

Different materials reflect, absorb, emit, or scatter electromagnetic energy differently.

This difference allows sensors to distinguish between different materials and surface conditions.

---

## Remote Sensing Sensors

A sensor detects electromagnetic energy and records information about it.

Different sensors are designed for different purposes.

Examples include:

- Optical imagers
- Thermal infrared imagers
- Radiometers
- Radar instruments
- Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR)
- Radar altimeters

The type of sensor affects what information can be measured.

---

## Image Resolution

Remote sensing images can be described using several types of resolution.

### Spatial Resolution

Spatial resolution describes the amount of ground represented by each pixel.

Smaller pixels generally allow smaller features to be distinguished.

Example:

A 10-meter spatial resolution means one pixel represents approximately a 10 m × 10 m area on the ground.

### Temporal Resolution

Temporal resolution describes how frequently a sensor can observe the same location.

A shorter revisit time means the location can be observed more frequently.

### Radiometric Resolution

Radiometric resolution describes how sensitive a sensor is to differences in the amount of detected energy.

It is related to how finely a sensor can distinguish differences in signal intensity.

### Spectral Resolution

Spectral resolution describes how finely a sensor can distinguish different wavelengths or wavelength ranges.

A sensor with multiple spectral bands can collect information from different portions of the electromagnetic spectrum.

## Orbits

Earth-observing satellites can operate in different types of orbits.

Examples include:

- Low Earth Orbit (LEO)
- Medium Earth Orbit (MEO)
- High Earth orbit
- Polar orbit
- Geostationary orbit
- Geosynchronous orbit

The orbit affects how frequently a satellite can observe a location, how large an area it can observe, and how the satellite moves relative to Earth.

## Mapping and Measurements

Remote sensing data can be displayed as maps and images.

Remote sensing can be used to measure or estimate:

- Distance
- Area
- Perimeter
- Elevation
- Slope
- Changes in land cover

Maps may include information such as:

- Scale
- Orientation
- Legend
- Coordinates
- Other marginal information

## Image Interpretation

Image interpretation means examining an image and using visual or measured information to understand what is represented.

Useful clues can include:

- Color
- Brightness
- Shape
- Size
- Texture
- Pattern
- Location
- Context

Different types of imagery can display the same area differently.

Examples include:

- Natural color imagery
- False color imagery
- Grayscale imagery
- Thermal imagery
- Radar imagery
- Elevation displays
- Classified maps

---

## Earth Observation

Remote sensing can help scientists observe changes and processes on Earth.

Examples include:

- Aerosols
- Surface temperature
- Albedo
- Vegetation changes
- Elevation changes
- Subsidence
- Geohazards
- Earth processes

Remote sensing observations can help scientists understand how Earth's surface and atmosphere change.

---

## Land Cover Classification

Land cover classification involves assigning areas of an image to categories representing the type of surface present.

Examples of land-cover categories include:

- Forest
- Water
- Urban areas
- Cropland
- Grassland
- Barren land

Two common approaches are:

### Supervised Classification

The user provides training information for known classes.

The classification system then uses those examples to classify other areas.

### Unsupervised Classification

The system groups pixels based on similarities in their measured characteristics.

The user can then interpret or assign meaning to the resulting groups.

---

## Human and Environmental Impacts

Remote sensing can be used to study changes caused by natural processes and human activities.

Examples include:

- Habitat loss
- Deforestation
- Urbanization
- Land-cover change
- Environmental damage
- Disaster damage

Comparing imagery from different dates can help identify changes over time.

---

## Important Idea

Remote sensing connects:

**Energy → Sensor → Data → Image/Measurement → Interpretation**

Understanding this sequence helps explain how scientists use remote sensing to learn about Earth.
