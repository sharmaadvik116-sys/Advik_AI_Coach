# Human and Environmental Impacts in Remote Sensing

## What Are Human and Environmental Impacts?

Remote sensing can be used to observe changes caused by both human activities and natural processes.

Scientists can use satellite and other remote sensing data to:

- Detect changes
- Map affected areas
- Measure the size of changes
- Compare conditions over time
- Monitor recovery
- Support environmental studies

---

# Habitat Loss

Habitat loss occurs when an area that provides living space and resources for organisms is reduced, changed, or destroyed.

Causes can include:

- Deforestation
- Urban development
- Agriculture
- Roads
- Mining
- Other land-cover changes

Remote sensing can help identify areas where habitat has changed.

---

# Deforestation

Deforestation is the removal or loss of forest cover.

Remote sensing can be used to:

- Detect forest loss
- Map the affected area
- Compare forest cover between dates
- Monitor changes over large regions

Satellite imagery is especially useful because forests can cover very large areas that are difficult to survey entirely from the ground.

---

# Detecting Deforestation

A simple remote sensing approach is to compare images collected at different times.

### Earlier image

A region may show extensive forest cover.

### Later image

The same region may show:

- Cleared land
- Agriculture
- Roads
- Buildings
- Other land-cover types

The difference between the observations can indicate land-cover change.

---

# Urbanization

Urbanization is the growth or expansion of developed areas.

Urban areas can include:

- Buildings
- Roads
- Parking lots
- Industrial areas
- Other developed surfaces

Remote sensing can help monitor urban growth by comparing imagery from different dates.

---

# Urban Growth Example

Suppose an earlier image shows:

- Forest
- Agricultural land
- A small city

A later image shows:

- More buildings
- More roads
- Less undeveloped land

This pattern may indicate urban expansion.

Remote sensing can help measure how much land changed.

---

# Land-Cover Change

Land cover can change because of both human activity and natural processes.

Examples include:

- Forest becoming agricultural land
- Agricultural land becoming urban land
- Forest becoming burned land
- Wetlands changing
- Vegetation recovering after disturbance

Remote sensing allows scientists to compare land cover at different times.

---

# Change Detection

Change detection is the process of comparing observations from different times to identify meaningful differences.

A basic change-detection workflow is:

1. Obtain an earlier observation.
2. Obtain a later observation.
3. Make sure the observations are appropriate for comparison.
4. Identify differences.
5. Determine what caused the differences when possible.
6. Map or measure the change.

---

# Why Dates Matter

When comparing remote sensing images, the date of each observation is important.

A change between two images may be caused by:

- Actual land-cover change
- Seasonal differences
- Different atmospheric conditions
- Different illumination conditions
- Different sensors
- Differences in image processing

Therefore, an observed difference does not automatically prove that a human activity caused the change.

---

# Environmental Damage Assessment

Remote sensing can help assess areas affected by environmental damage.

Examples include:

- Wildfires
- Floods
- Storm damage
- Deforestation
- Landslides
- Volcanic activity
- Other disturbances

Remote sensing can provide information about the location and extent of affected areas.

---

# Wildfire Damage

Wildfires can change vegetation and land surface characteristics.

Remote sensing can help identify:

- Burned areas
- Vegetation loss
- Thermal anomalies
- Smoke
- Changes before and after a fire

Comparing imagery before and after a fire can help estimate the affected area.

---

# Flood Damage

Flooding can change the appearance and measured characteristics of land.

Remote sensing can help:

- Map flooded areas
- Estimate the extent of flooding
- Compare conditions before and after a flood
- Monitor changes as water levels change

Radar can be especially useful because it can collect data without relying on sunlight.

---

# Landslides

Landslides can cause significant changes to Earth's surface.

Remote sensing can help identify:

- Changes in terrain
- Disturbed areas
- Surface movement
- Changes in vegetation
- Changes in elevation

InSAR can be useful for detecting surface deformation associated with ground movement.

---

# Surface Deformation

Some human activities and natural processes can cause Earth's surface to move.

Examples include:

- Groundwater withdrawal
- Resource extraction
- Earthquakes
- Volcanic activity
- Soil compaction

InSAR can compare radar observations to detect changes in the position of the Earth's surface.

---

# Subsidence

Subsidence is the downward movement of Earth's surface.

Possible causes include:

- Groundwater withdrawal
- Underground resource extraction
- Soil compaction
- Natural geological processes

Remote sensing can help detect and measure patterns of subsidence.

---

# Monitoring Environmental Change

Repeated remote sensing observations can be used to monitor environmental change.

Scientists may compare:

- Different seasons
- Different years
- Before and after an event
- Different land-cover conditions

This creates a time series of observations.

---

# Multi-Temporal Analysis

Multi-temporal analysis means studying observations collected at different times.

For example:

### Image 1

January 2025

### Image 2

January 2026

Comparing the two observations can help identify changes that occurred during the year.

Using similar dates can sometimes reduce differences caused by seasonal conditions.

---

# Remote Sensing and Human Activities

Human activities can change Earth's surface.

Examples include:

- Agriculture
- Mining
- Construction
- Urbanization
- Deforestation
- Road building
- Water-resource use

Remote sensing can provide evidence of these changes by showing how the surface changes over time.

---

# Agriculture

Remote sensing can help monitor agricultural areas.

Possible observations include:

- Crop growth
- Crop condition
- Irrigated areas
- Land-cover changes
- Harvest patterns

Different crops and vegetation conditions can produce different spectral responses.

---

# Mining

Mining can significantly change land cover and terrain.

Remote sensing can help identify:

- Mine sites
- Excavated areas
- Tailings
- Vegetation loss
- Changes in surface shape

Repeated observations can help monitor changes around mining areas.

---

# Roads and Infrastructure

Road construction and other infrastructure can change land cover.

Remote sensing can help map:

- Roads
- Buildings
- Construction areas
- Developed land

Changes in these features can be detected by comparing observations over time.

---

# Environmental Monitoring

Remote sensing is useful for monitoring environmental conditions because it can provide:

- Large-area coverage
- Repeated observations
- Multiple wavelengths
- Different types of measurements
- Information about areas that are difficult to access

However, remote sensing observations must be interpreted carefully.

---

# Evidence and Interpretation

When interpreting environmental changes, separate:

### Observation

What the remote sensing data actually show.

Example:

"A previously vegetated area appears to have less vegetation in the later image."

### Interpretation

What the observation may mean.

Example:

"The area may have experienced vegetation loss."

### Possible Cause

Why the change may have occurred.

Example:

"The change could be related to development, agriculture, fire, or another disturbance."

A remote sensing image alone may not always identify the exact cause.

---

# Accuracy Considerations

When analyzing environmental change, consider:

- Sensor type
- Spatial resolution
- Temporal resolution
- Spectral information
- Image date
- Atmospheric conditions
- Seasonal differences
- Cloud cover
- Image processing
- Classification accuracy

These factors can affect how confidently a change can be identified.

---

# Human Impact vs. Natural Change

Not every environmental change is caused by people.

Possible natural causes include:

- Floods
- Wildfires
- Earthquakes
- Volcanoes
- Landslides
- Seasonal vegetation changes

Possible human causes include:

- Deforestation
- Urbanization
- Agriculture
- Mining
- Construction

Remote sensing provides observations that can help investigate these changes.

---

# Remote Sensing Workflow for Environmental Impacts

A useful workflow is:

### Step 1

Identify the environmental question.

### Step 2

Choose appropriate remote sensing data.

### Step 3

Check the sensor and wavelengths.

### Step 4

Check spatial and temporal resolution.

### Step 5

Compare observations.

### Step 6

Identify measurable changes.

### Step 7

Interpret the evidence.

### Step 8

Consider alternative explanations.

### Step 9

Map or measure the affected area.

# Key Memory Strategy

When analyzing an environmental change:

**Observe → Compare → Measure → Interpret → Consider causes**

Remember:

**Remote sensing shows evidence of change.**

The scientist must then use the available evidence and context to determine what the change may represent.
